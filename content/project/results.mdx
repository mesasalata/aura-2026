<Section id="fet" title="FET performance" kicker="04">

The field-effect transistor is designed so that catapult opening moves charge away from the gate and ticks current between source and drain. Characterisation includes time-course traces, residual after electrode subtraction, and comparison of catapult variants.

<Section id="device" title="Siphon & electrode prototypes" kicker="05">

Inline siphon chambers were built to test whether a milking pulse can wash the FET surface without another trip to the lab. Reference electrodes sit upstream of the chamber on every prototype.

<Placeholder label="Siphon prototype photos and electrode placement - data pending" />

Early bench tests focused on flow through the chamber and electrode stability before running biological samples.

</Section>

<Section id="comparison" title="Comparison to reference methods" kicker="06">

The ultimate question is whether AURA's flag moves earlier or more accessibly than methods farms already use. We frame comparison against:

- Somatic cell count (industry standard early indicator)
- Electrical conductivity (common inline proxy)
- Culture or PCR (confirmatory reference)

<Placeholder label="Side-by-side comparison table - pending paired samples" />

We do not claim clinical validation. Any comparison is exploratory and sample-limited. AURA is a flag, not a diagnosis.

</Section>
