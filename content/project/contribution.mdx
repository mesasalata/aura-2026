<Section id="overview" title="Why contribution matters" kicker="01">

iGEM teams stand on each other's work. AURA is one season of design and testing - what we document, deposit, and leave behind should make the next team's path shorter, whether they continue mastitis biosensing or fork the DNA catapult for another inline diagnostic.

<Callout variant="note" title="What we mean by contribution">
  Parts on the Registry, reproducible protocols, honest negative results, and design frameworks that transfer - not just a polished story.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section id="parts" title="Parts & constructs" kicker="02">

We aim to submit characterised and uncharacterised parts relevant to inline FET biosensing:

- **DNA catapult stems** with invasion regions complementary to bta-miR-223
- **Off-target invasion controls** so a hit is not just "any RNA"
- **Composite gate units** combining catapult and FET-surface chemistry (where assembly is complete)

Each part will include sequence, intended use, and measurement data where available. Parts without full characterisation are labelled accordingly - future teams should not have to guess what was validated.

<Section id="protocols" title="Protocols & methods" kicker="03">

Reproducibility is a contribution in itself. We document:

- Milk sample preparation and miR-223 spiking for assay development
- Catapult-opening workflows and controls
- FET current traces and residual calculation
- Reference-electrode placement and baseline procedure

Protocols live on the Protocols and Notebook pages with enough detail for another lab to repeat the work, including what failed and why.

</Section>

<Section id="framework" title="Design framework" kicker="04">

Not everything transfers as a deposited oligo. We also contribute a **decision framework** for early mastitis biosensor design:

1. **Marker selection** - score candidates on timing, specificity, and deployability (we chose miR-223)
2. **Matrix compatibility** - test in milk early, not only in buffer; ionic milk needs its own baseline
3. **Readout first** - define what the farmer must see (a flag, not a courtroom diagnosis) before optimising molecular detail
4. **Stakeholder alignment** - farmers and vets set constraints: sit in the milking line, cost less than the loss, a readout before the tank is mixed

<Section id="lessons" title="Lessons for future teams" kicker="07">

What we would tell the next team on day one:

- **Scope to the season.** A credible proof-of-concept beats an overambitious pipeline with no data.
- **Test in matrix early.** Milk is not PBS; assumptions from buffer-only assays rarely survive.
- **Design for the readout.** Farmers need a quiet flag in the parlour, not a fluorescent workbench.
- **Mark pending work.** Judges and peers respect honesty; implied validation erodes trust.
- **Talk to users before locking the construct.** Our best design changes came from farmers and vets, not from the bench alone.

</Section>
