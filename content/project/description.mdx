<Section id="overview" title="AURA: an inline biosensor for earlier mastitis detection" kicker="01">

AURA focuses on creating an easy-to-use diagnostic system for subclinical mastitis in the dairy industry. By detecting mastitis earlier, our system aims to help farmers care for their animals, reduce unnecessary antibiotic use, support more informed treatment decisions, and decrease the economic losses associated with mastitis.

Our proposed tool is an inline biosensor using field-effect transistor (FET) technology to detect changes in bta-miR-223, a microRNA associated with the inflammatory response to mastitis.

</Section>

<Section id="mirna" title="Detecting miR-223" kicker="02">

bta-miR-223 is involved in regulating inflammation and is found at altered levels in milk during mastitis. Because these molecular changes can occur before obvious clinical symptoms are visible, miR-223 provides a promising target for earlier monitoring of udder health.

Detecting miR-223 could therefore give farmers an earlier warning that a cow may be developing mastitis, allowing the animal to be investigated sooner and helping treatment decisions to be made more efficiently.

</Section>

<Section id="fet" title="From molecular recognition to an electrical signal" kicker="03">

The FET surface would be functionalised with a DNA catapult containing a region complementary to miR-223. When miR-223 binds to this recognition region, hybridisation changes the structure of the DNA probe and causes the catapult to open.

This conformational change alters the distribution of electrical charge close to the FET surface. The transistor detects this change through its electrical response, converting the molecular interaction into an electronic signal that can be measured.

A reference electrode would help establish stable electrical conditions in the liquid sample, which is particularly important because milk contains high concentrations of ions that can influence FET measurements.

</Section>

<Section id="farmer" title="From the sensor to the farmer" kicker="04">

The electrical measurements would be transferred to the UdderNet software platform. UdderNet combines the sensor reading with additional cow and milk data, processes the information, displays the result, generates reports, and can alert farm staff when a cow may require further attention.

</Section>

<Section id="milking" title="Integration into milking" kicker="05">

During each milking session, a small portion of milk would be directed into a separate sensing chamber containing the biosensor. The milk would pass over the sensing surface, allowing AURA to monitor individual cows as part of the normal milking process.

Repeated measurements could allow farmers to follow changes in each cow over time without requiring a separate test for every measurement. The system is designed to be integrated into existing milking infrastructure and to require minimal additional input from the farmer.

The same sensing concept could also be adapted to other nucleic-acid biomarkers by changing the recognition sequence of the DNA probe, creating the possibility of more specific diagnostic systems in the future.

</Section>

<Section id="progress" title="Our progress" kicker="06">

This year, we adapted the DNA catapult to recognise miR-223, optimised the probe through several engineering cycles, demonstrated detection of synthetic miR-223 RNA at 39 °C, investigated methods for resetting the sensor, and developed the software and hardware architecture required for an integrated AURA system.

The next stages are direct FET-based detection, testing in raw milk, and integration of the complete system into the milking workflow.

</Section>
