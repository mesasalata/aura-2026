<Section id="overview" title="Engineering overview" kicker="01">

We treated this as a farm tool first, a circuit second. Engineering is five Design–Build–Test–Learn cycles that start in the parlour: sit in the milking line, cost less than the loss they prevent, and return a flag anyone can act on before the tank is mixed.

That ruled out culture and PCR as the everyday test. The loops that remain are how we read **bta-miR-223** on a FET without a fluorescent workbench.

<Callout variant="note" title="How to read this page">
  Cycles describe intent, design rationale, and what we tested or plan to test. Where wet-lab data is still pending, we say so explicitly.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section id="cycle-1" title="Cycle 1 - Target: miR-223" kicker="02">

**Design.** Farmers and vets needed something earlier than a swollen udder and more specific than a generic inflammation count. Literature on host miRNAs in milk pointed at **bta-miR-223**, which rises to control bacterial inflammation before physiological changes show.

**Build.** A decision matrix scored SCC, conductivity, culture, PCR, and miR-223 on timing, bacterial specificity, and whether the test could live in the line.

**Test.** We compared published miR-223 windows against SCC kinetics from mastitis challenge studies.

**Learn.** A hit on miR-223 can mean bacterial subclinical mastitis, not just “something’s off.” Cycle 2 asks how to catch that molecule on a surface.

</Section>

<Section id="cycle-2" title="Cycle 2 - DNA catapult" kicker="03">

**Design.** A DNA catapult on the FET surface carries an invasion region complementary to the miR-223 strand. Association should open the stem: the negative backbone of the miRNA repels the invasion region from the complementary DNA stem.

**Build.** Design and immobilise catapult oligos with a defined invasion region; control sequences that should not open on off-target miRNAs.

**Test.** Binding and opening in buffer, then in milk matrix, against miR-223 and irrelevant RNA.

**Learn.** Milk is ionic and noisy. Opening still has to move charge far enough from the surface for the FET to notice - which is cycle 3.

<Section id="cycle-3" title="Cycle 3 - FET current" kicker="04">

**Design.** No fluorescent workbench. When the invasion region moves away from the biosensor surface, the local electrical field changes. The FET reads that as current between source and drain.

**Build.** Couple catapults to a FET gate; define source–drain bias and sampling window per milking siphon.

**Test.** Current traces with and without miR-223; spiked milk versus unspiked.

**Learn.** Highly ionic milk can swamp the surface field. Reference electrodes go upstream of the chamber so we can subtract the sample’s own salt.

<Section id="cycle-4" title="Cycle 4 - Inline siphon & electrodes" kicker="05">

**Design.** During each milking, siphon a small sample into a chamber that washes the FET. Place reference electrodes before the chamber to register ionic concentration and electrochemical activity, then calibrate the FET residual.

**Build.** Chamber and electrode layout meant to integrate into milking machines at construction, with minimal upkeep.

**Test.** Flow, fouling, and electrode baselines on milk-like matrices; temperature variation in a parlour range.

**Learn.** Hardware and wet-lab ran together: the siphon sets how long the catapult has to work, and the electrodes set what “zero” means for that cow that milking.

</Section>

<Section id="cycle-5" title="Cycle 5 - Flag, not verdict" kicker="06">

**Design.** After each reading, compare FET current to the sample’s own electrode baseline. A small on-farm model turns the residual into a miR-223 load score. One noisy spike is not a verdict.

**Build.** Threshold logic: if the score stays high across consecutive sessions, the parlour computer marks the animal and sends a quiet alert to the farmer’s phone or the parlour display.

**Test.** Simulated milking series - noisy singles versus persistent highs - with veterinary input on what is actionable.

**Learn.** Human Practices kept the last mile honest: look at this cow, consider confirmatory testing, skip blanket treatment of the herd.
