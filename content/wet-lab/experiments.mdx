<Section id="methodology" title="Methodology" kicker="01">

This experiment was designed to test the efficacy of different probe candidates at binding to the target miRNA. Rather than using FET sensor technology, we used BHQ-1 (Black Hole Quencher) and a FAM-6 fluorophore to create a proof of concept without designing the entire system. Both attachments were placed at either end of the catapult sequence, so when it is closed we get no signal, but when it opens a signal will be produced as the BHQ is separated from the fluorophore. Alongside this, we also replaced the miRNA 223 with a strand of ssDNA with the same sequence, thus eliminating a layer of complexity. We did this because DNA is more stable than RNA.

### Reaction conditions

Catapult probes were diluted to a **200 nM working concentration**, corresponding to a **100 nM final probe concentration** in the assay.

Reactions were performed in **1× PBS supplemented with 5 mM MgCl₂**.

MgCl₂ was included to support stabilization of the probe secondary structure and reduce unwanted opening of the hairpin in the absence of target.

Final reaction volumes were either **20 µL or 40 µL per well**.

Fluorescence was measured for **120 minutes at 30.3°C**.

</Section>

<Section id="refold" title="Refold the hairpin beacon" kicker="03">

**Volume planning for one 384-well plate:**

384 wells × 20 µL × 1.2 overage = **9,216 µL of 200 nM beacon working stock**.

Refold a concentrated batch (1,200 µL at 1 µM) and dilute 1:5 on the day:

- 240 µL of 10 µM beacon working stock + 960 µL of 1× binding buffer → 1,200 µL at 1 µM
- On assay day: dilute the 1 µM refolded stock 1:5 in 1× binding buffer to make 200 nM

**Note:** 384-well plates use 40 µL total per well (20 µL beacon + 20 µL target), not 100 µL. All 2× stocks and volume calculations below reflect this.

**Refolding steps:**

1. Add 960 µL of 1× binding buffer to a low-binding 1.5 mL amber tube.
2. Add 240 µL of 10 µM beacon working stock.
3. Mix by gentle inversion 10×.
4. **Thermocycler:**
   1. 95 °C × 5 min
   2. Ramp to 25 °C at 0.1 °C/s (~11.7 min)
   3. Hold 25 °C × 5 min
   4. Hold 4 °C indefinitely
5. **Heat block (fallback):**
   1. 95 °C × 5 min
   2. Turn off the block
   3. Cool passively on the bench
6. Wrap in foil. Use within 24 h if stored at 4 °C, or aliquot and freeze at −20 °C.

<Section id="dbtl" title="Design–Build–Test–Learn" kicker="09">

These experiments formed an iterative engineering cycle.

**Design.** We designed several variants of the original miR-223 catapult probe by modifying specific structural regions.

**Build.** The oligonucleotides were synthesized with a BHQ-1 quencher and 6-FAM fluorophore and prepared at standardized working concentrations.

**Test.** Each probe was tested against synthetic DNA target analogues using time-course fluorescence measurements.

**Learn.** Probe structure had a major effect on assay performance. The original miR-223 v2 probe produced an SNR of 6.73, while the optimized 3′ end-shortened probe reached an SNR of 17.96.

This result informed our subsequent design decisions and identified the **3′ end 2 bp shorter probe** as the lead architecture for continued development.

</Section>

<Section id="limitations" title="Limitations" kicker="10">

Although the results demonstrate successful optimization of the fluorescent probe system, several limitations remain.

First, these experiments were performed using **synthetic DNA analogues rather than native miRNA molecules**. The assay must therefore be validated using RNA targets before conclusions can be made regarding direct miRNA detection.

Second, the experiments were conducted under controlled buffer conditions. Milk is a complex biological matrix containing proteins, lipids, ions, nucleases, and other molecules that may affect probe folding and fluorescence.

</Section>

<Section id="conclusion" title="Conclusion" kicker="11">

Our results demonstrate that relatively small changes to the catapult-probe architecture can strongly influence assay performance.

The **3′ end 2 bp shorter** design consistently produced the strongest signal relative to background, suggesting that shortening this region improved the accessibility or displacement behavior of the probe.

One possible explanation is that shortening the 3′ region reduces the energetic barrier required for target-mediated strand displacement. However, this mechanism was not measured directly and therefore remains a hypothesis requiring further experimental validation.

The 3′ end 2 bp shorter not only proved to be the most successful probe, it did so by a relatively large margin, and with an SNR of 17.96, it had an approximately **2.7-fold improvement in SNR** compared to the baseline probe.

</Section>
