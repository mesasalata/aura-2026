<Section id="milk" title="Milk sample preparation" kicker="02">

**Purpose.** Prepare consistent milk matrix for spiking and assay development.

**Outline.**
1. Collect or thaw milk aliquots; record source and date
2. Centrifuge to remove fat if required for assay format
3. Filter or dilute per assay SOP; document dilution factor
4. Spike miR-223 at defined concentrations; include unspiked control

<Callout variant="lab" title="Matrix matters">
  Buffer-only controls are necessary but not sufficient - always include unspiked and spiked milk. Milk is ionic; that is why the reference electrodes exist.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section id="binding" title="Catapult opening screen" kicker="03">

**Purpose.** Compare DNA-catapult candidates against purified miR-223 and milk matrix.

**Outline.**
1. Immobilise candidate catapult on the FET gate (or a screening surface)
2. Incubate with miR-223 in buffer, then in diluted milk
3. Wash; include irrelevant-RNA control
4. Read opening (shape change / current) against the closed-stem baseline

<Section id="fet" title="FET current traces" kicker="04">

**Purpose.** Turn catapult opening into source–drain current.

**Outline.**
1. Mount the gated surface; record closed-stem baseline in unspiked milk
2. Introduce miR-223 spike or field sample
3. Record current between source and drain through the milking-pulse window
4. Subtract that run's reference-electrode baseline to get the residual

</Section>

<Section id="electrodes" title="Reference electrodes" kicker="05">

**Purpose.** Taste this sample's salt and electrochemical noise before the chamber, so ionic milk is less likely to raise a false flag.

**Outline.**
1. Place electrodes upstream of the siphon chamber
2. Register ionic concentration and electrochemical activity for that pulse
3. Store the baseline with the FET trace for the same cow and milking
4. Feed both into the on-farm load score

</Section>
