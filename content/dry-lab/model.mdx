<Section id="overview" title="Modeling overview" kicker="01">

The on-farm model is small on purpose. After each reading it compares FET current with a baseline from that same sample’s reference electrodes, turns the residual into a **miR-223 load score** for that cow and that milking, and only flags when the score stays high. Models are theoretical until calibrated with wet-lab data.

</Section>

<Section id="baseline" title="Sample-wise baseline" kicker="02">

Milk’s salt and electrochemical noise are measured just upstream of the chamber. The FET current is not judged against a herd average - it is judged against **this sample**. That is how we offset highly ionic conditions on the biosensor surface.

<Section id="score" title="Load score" kicker="03">

The residual becomes a miR-223 load score. Sensitivity analysis asks which terms dominate: electrode noise, siphon timing, catapult occupancy, and how sticky the FET gate is after a string of milkings.

<ScienceExplainer
  accent="signal"
  analogy="Not a smoke alarm that screams at steam - a score that asks whether this cow, this week, is still high after you subtract the milk she always has."
  precise="The residual is FET current minus a function of the reference-electrode ionic and electrochemical reading for the same siphoned sample."
/>

</Section>

<Section id="thresholds" title="Consecutive sessions, not one spike" kicker="04">

One noisy spike is not a verdict. If the score stays above a threshold across consecutive sessions, the parlour computer marks the animal. Veterinary input sets what is actionable; the model only proposes a region.

</Section>

<Section id="limits" title="Limits" kicker="05">

<Callout variant="warn" title="Not a substitute for data">
  Models without calibration are hypotheses. We do not use unvalidated model output as evidence of clinical performance. The message to the farmer is a flag, not a diagnosis.
</Callout>

</Section>
