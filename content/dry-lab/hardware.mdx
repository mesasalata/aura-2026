<Section id="motivation" title="Motivation" kicker="01">

<Callout title="Proposed design, not built">

This page documents the proposed hardware architecture for AURA. The mechanical and electronic system was designed conceptually and in CAD, but the complete integrated hardware was not constructed or experimentally validated during this iGEM season.

</Callout>

Our hardware design addresses these functions through two connected modules: a sampling assembly that controls liquid delivery and a sensing assembly that converts the response into electronic measurements. Together, these form the hardware concept for AURA.

The design separates the main milk passage from the sampling pathway. A portion of liquid would enter a dedicated sensing chamber, while the main passage remains available for through-flow. Measurements would then be transmitted to the app, avoiding the need for an onboard screen.

</Section>

<Section id="overview" title="System Overview" kicker="02">

The system is organised into four functional stages:

1. Sample admission through a sliding-sleeve valve.
2. Contact with the DNA-probe FET inside a sensing chamber.
3. Electrical acquisition through a readout circuit and microcontroller.
4. Data transfer to the app, followed by sample release and rinsing.

<Section id="sampling" title="Sampling Module" kicker="03">

### Design Objective

The sampling module is designed to admit liquid into the sensing pathway without deliberately closing the main milk route. It combines a branched fitting with two control mechanisms: an internal sliding sleeve for sample admission and an external pinch valve for outlet control.

This division gives each mechanism a specific task rather than requiring one valve to manage the entire fluid-handling sequence.

### Mechanical Layout

The fitting consists of a straight main passage and an angled side branch. Its overall length is approximately 100 mm from tip to tip. Barbed connectors fit inside silicone tubing to connect the assembly to the surrounding fluid pathway.

The side branch leads toward the sensing chamber. A hollow sleeve inside the main fitting controls access to the branch entrance. Downstream of the chamber, a screw-operated pinch valve controls the flexible outlet tube.

### Sliding-Sleeve Valve

The sampling valve operates by translating a hollow internal sleeve along the main fitting.

In the closed position, the sleeve wall covers the side-branch entrance. Liquid would continue through the central passage toward the main outlet.

In the open position, the sleeve moves away from the branch entrance. The exposed opening allows liquid to enter the sampling pathway when a suitable pressure difference is present. Figure 2 illustrates these two positions.

<WikiFigure
  src="/images/Hardware%20Figure%203.png"
  width="lg"
  caption="Longitudinal cross-section showing the branch entrance covered and exposed by translation of the hollow sleeve. The main passage remains open in the intended arrangement."

An external slider connection would move the sleeve between defined positions. The mechanical design would incorporate retention, travel limits, and sealing around the sleeve and actuator-access feature.

### Outlet Pinch Valve

The pinch valve controls flow through the silicone outlet tubing by external compression. It comprises a rigid holder, screw, pressing pad, and fixed opposing support.

Tightening the screw advances the pad toward the tube. The tube flattens until its inner walls meet, closing the liquid pathway. Loosening the screw releases compression and allows the lumen to reopen.

Figure 3 shows the valve perpendicular to the tube's length.

<WikiFigure
  src="/images/Hardware%20Figure%204.png"
  width="lg"
  caption="Screw-operated compression of flexible silicone tubing. The pressing pad closes the lumen against a fixed support and withdraws to permit reopening."
/>

The valve mechanism remains outside the fluid pathway. Its holder provides space for the tube to flatten sideways, while the smooth pad distributes the applied force.

### Sample Transport and Rinsing

The valves determine whether pathways are open; sample movement depends on the driving force and pressure conditions.

Gravity feed is the initial delivery concept, with pump-driven flow or controlled suction as alternatives. During filling, the outlet provides a route for displaced liquid or air.

A separate sampling pathway creates an opportunity to control flow into the chamber independently of the main line.

After measurement, the sample would be directed to waste. A separate rinse connection would introduce compatible liquid to displace the previous sample from the chamber and tubing. This connection would be arranged to prevent rinse liquid or sensor-contacted sample from returning to the main milk collection.

### CAD and Materials

The mechanical assembly is designed in Fusion. Dimensioned drawings would document bore diameters, wall thicknesses, sleeve travel, tubing interfaces, and screw geometry.

PLA is being considered for an initial geometric print. Selection of sample-contact materials would additionally account for cleaning, chemical compatibility, surface finish, and access to internal passages.

The external pinch-valve holder and the wetted fitting have different material requirements because only the latter directly contacts the sample.

</Section>

<Section id="sensing" title="Sensing Module" kicker="04">

### Design Objective

The sensing module is designed to bring a controlled sample into contact with a DNA-probe FET and acquire an electrical response. It combines a fluidic chamber, protected chip connections, and an electronic reader.

The chamber establishes the liquid–sensor interface, while the reader establishes electrical operating conditions and records the response.

### Sensing Chamber

The chamber contains an inlet, liquid cavity, outlet, and chip mount. Its fluid-contact window exposes the probe-functionalised sensing region while keeping external electrical contacts outside the wet cavity.

Figure 4 shows the intended separation between liquid handling and electrical connections.

<WikiFigure

The housing would support repeatable chip positioning. Seals around the fluid-contact window would confine the sample, while the inlet and outlet would guide liquid across the sensing area and allow displacement during filling and rinsing.

For a liquid-gated implementation, a reference electrode would contact the solution and connect to the gate-bias circuit.

### Electronic Architecture

The electronics are organised into three hardware blocks:

- DNA-probe FET.
- Sensor readout circuit.
- Microcontroller.

A regulated supply powers the hardware. The microcontroller communicates measurements to the app, which performs processing, storage, and presentation. Figure 5 shows these relationships.

### FET Interface

The DNA probe provides molecular recognition through its intended interaction with complementary miR-223 RNA. The FET provides the electrical transduction interface.

The sensing approach would investigate whether target binding near the active region produces a measurable change in transistor behaviour. The electrical response would be interpreted in the context of the operating conditions and target-recognition system.

### Readout Circuit

The readout circuit establishes the FET's bias conditions and measures its electrical response.

Figure 5 distinguishes these functions through two connections: the measurement path from the FET to the readout and the bias-control path back to the FET.

The acquisition front end would condition the signal for digitisation. Its measurement range, gain, and filtering would be matched to the selected transistor and expected response.

### Microcontroller and Power

The microcontroller would coordinate acquisition, associate readings with timestamps, and communicate them to the app.

Digitisation could use an internal ADC or additional conversion circuitry. The architecture leaves this implementation open.

A regulated supply would support the reader and controller. Sensitive measurement paths would be arranged separately from any future actuator-power wiring.

</Section>

<Section id="app" title="Hardware–App Communication" kicker="05">

The app serves as the user-facing part of the system, so the sensing hardware does not require an onboard display.

The data connection would carry sensor readings and associated measurement information. Its implementation would be selected according to the final device arrangement.

The intended app functions include:

- Displaying the sensor response over time.
- Associating readings with sample identifiers.
- Comparing measurements with the starting baseline.
- Recording sample and rinse events.
- Saving raw readings alongside processed outputs.
- Presenting results with their measurement context.

This separation keeps the hardware focused on acquisition and communication while the app handles interpretation and presentation.

</Section>

<Section id="operation" title="Operating Principle" kicker="06">

The device would use a coordinated sample–measure–rinse sequence.

### 1. Prepare

A compatible measurement solution establishes the chamber's starting condition. The reader records the initial electrical signal.

### 2. Admit the Sample

The sliding sleeve exposes the sampling branch. With an appropriate driving force and outlet pathway, sample enters the chamber and displaces existing liquid or air.

### 3. Stabilise

Sample admission is closed, and the outlet is controlled according to the measurement procedure. Delivery stops before the chamber is isolated.

### 4. Measure

The readout circuit acquires the FET response. The microcontroller transfers readings to the app with timestamps and event information.

### 5. Release and Rinse

The outlet pathway opens to waste, and rinse liquid enters through the separate connection to displace the previous sample.

### 6. Record and Store

The reader records the post-rinse condition, and the app stores the measurement sequence and settings.

Flushing the fluid pathway and restoring a target-bound probe surface are distinct operations. Any surface-regeneration procedure would depend on the selected sensing chemistry.

</Section>
