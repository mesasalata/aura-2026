<Section id="team" title="Team safety guidelines" kicker="01">

All team members who worked in the laboratory completed the required EPFL FOBS 1 + 2 safety training before beginning experimental work.<sup>[1]</sup>

During laboratory work, we followed standard safety procedures:<sup>[2,3]</sup>

- Laboratory coats, gloves, and protective eyewear were worn when appropriate.
- Long hair was tied back and food and drinks were prohibited in the laboratory.
- Work surfaces were disinfected before and after experiments.
- Pipette tips were changed between samples to reduce contamination.
- Reagents and samples were clearly labelled and stored correctly.
- Equipment was checked before use and operated according to laboratory instructions.
- Laboratory materials were sterilized or disposed of according to EPFL procedures.

</Section>

<Section id="project" title="Project safety" kicker="02">

AURA was developed as a cell-free, in vitro biosensing project. Our experiments used synthetic DNA and RNA oligonucleotides, RNase H, streptavidin beads, PBS, MgCl₂, and fluorophore/quencher-labelled probes.

We did not culture pathogenic organisms, perform animal testing, or release biological material into the environment. Our experimental work also did not involve raw or infected milk.

Sterilization and careful sample handling were used throughout the project to reduce contamination and maintain a clean working environment.

</Section>

<Section id="responsible-use" title="Responsible use" kicker="03">

AURA is intended as a screening tool rather than an independent veterinary diagnosis. A positive signal would indicate that further investigation may be needed and should not independently determine treatment or antibiotic use.

The complete sensor would also require further validation in raw milk and farm conditions before real-world use.

</Section>
